About

Understanding compact objects through their response.

My research concerns the analytical gravitational-wave dynamics of compact objects. I am particularly interested in how black holes and neutron stars respond to external perturbations, and in the finite-size information that can be extracted from gravitational-wave scattering.

I connect modern scattering-amplitude methods and worldline effective field theory with black-hole and stellar perturbation theory. This provides a systematic route from the internal physics of compact objects to observable effects in binary dynamics and gravitational-wave signals.

Research

Current directions

Analytical tools for strong gravity and precision gravitational-wave physics.

01

Tidal response

Conservative and dissipative tidal effects in black holes and neutron stars, including dynamical tides, resonances and horizon absorption.

02

Amplitudes and EFT

Scattering amplitudes and worldline effective field theory as a bridge between compact-object structure and binary dynamics.

03

Strong-field dynamics

Quasinormal modes, gravitational-wave scattering and strong-field lensing as probes of black holes and horizonless compact objects.
